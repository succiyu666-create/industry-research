# Evidence and Quality Protocol

Use this protocol when the analysis depends on external research, current facts, quantitative claims, regulation, or consequential decisions.

## Source routing

Prefer the source closest to the underlying event or measurement:

1. Law, regulator, government statistics, court or standards body.
2. Company filing, audited statement, investor material, official product or pricing documentation.
3. Industry association, academic paper, or transparent dataset.
4. Reputable research or journalism with named sources and disclosed methodology.
5. Reviews, forums, interviews, and social evidence for customer language and experience—not for unsupported market totals.

Search results, aggregators, mirrors, and repeated articles derived from one origin do not constitute independent confirmation.

## Claim ledger

Track material claims with this minimum schema:

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Claim ID | Stable identifier |
| Claim | Specific, falsifiable statement |
| Type | Fact, inference, hypothesis, forecast, or attributed opinion |
| Period | Date or period covered |
| Geography | Market represented |
| Unit and definition | What exactly was measured |
| Source | Direct link or document identity |
| Origin | Entity that generated the underlying data |
| Evidence tier | Primary, near-primary, or secondary |
| Confidence | High, medium, or low, with reason |
| Gap or conflict | Missing evidence or competing claim |

## Numbers

Never present an important number without its year, geography, unit, and definition. For market estimates, preserve the method when available. Keep top-down and bottom-up calculations separate; treat agreement as triangulation, not proof.

If estimates conflict, test differences in:

- Included products or participants.
- Revenue versus transaction value, production value, installed base, or cumulative funding.
- Nominal versus real values and currency conversion.
- Calendar versus fiscal period.
- Geography and channel coverage.
- Historical actual versus vendor forecast.

## Independence and contradiction

Two pages that reproduce one press release are one origin. For high-impact claims, seek a second independent origin or explicitly state that independent verification was unavailable.

Do not average incompatible figures. Explain the contradiction, show how each figure was constructed, and state which decision each can or cannot support.

## Confidence

- **High** — direct, current, well-defined evidence from a primary source, independently corroborated when the claim warrants it.
- **Medium** — credible but indirect, older, incomplete, or dependent on a disclosed assumption.
- **Low** — sparse, self-reported, opaque, inferred from proxies, or materially disputed.

Confidence applies to a claim, not to a source in the abstract.

## Completion gate

Before delivering, check:

- The industry boundary matches the quantitative denominator.
- Payer, user, and beneficiary are not silently conflated.
- Cash, value, revenue, profit, and risk flows are distinct.
- Power claims identify a mechanism, not just a market-share rank.
- Risk analysis names both first and ultimate bearers.
- Change claims show a causal path and observable indicator.
- Important uncertainty remains visible in the conclusion.

When evidence is insufficient, return a partial model and a targeted verification plan. Do not compensate with confident prose.
